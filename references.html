Site reference table

Latitude and longitude are decimal degrees, east-positive, −180 to 180. All coordinates come from the published sources listed below. A site-level position means the exact spot of the object was not surveyed, so it is placed at the Lunar Module of its site. Column headers sort the table.

Map references

The globe in The Map Explorer is an orthographic view with north up, drawn directly from these mosaics. Its optional grid lines are a latitude and longitude graticule.

Moon

Mosaic
CGI Moon Kit color map, 2025 version
Instrument
Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera, Wide Angle Camera (LROC WAC)
Source
NASA SVS 4720 · file lroc_color_16bit_srgb_4k.tif
Credit
NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio (visualizer Ernie Wright, scientist Noah Petro); LROC WAC Color Mosaic, Arizona State University
Projection
Equirectangular, centered on 0° longitude
Frame
East-positive lunar longitude. NASA SVS does not state a datum for this product; the object coordinates plotted on it are Mean Earth/Polar Axis.
Resolution
4096 × 2048 px, about 2.7 km per pixel at the equator
Processing
A processed visualization product, which NASA describes as optimized for aesthetics, not science. Adapted from the Hapke-normalized WAC mosaic using the 643, 566 and 415 nm bands, with exposure and white balance adjusted toward human vision. Latitudes beyond 70° are filled from a lower-resolution monochrome laser-altimeter albedo map, and small high-latitude dropouts are inpainted. Converted here from 16-bit TIFF to 8-bit JPEG. No lighting is added on this page.

Mars

Mosaic
Viking MDIM 2.1 colorized global mosaic
Instrument
Viking Orbiter images; colorized by NASA Ames
Source
USGS Astrogeology product · copy used: Wikimedia Commons 1 km JPEG (public domain)
Credit
NASA / JPL / USGS
Projection
Simple cylindrical (equirectangular) on a 3396.19 km sphere, −180° to 180°, centered on 0° longitude
Frame
Planetocentric latitude, east-positive longitude
Resolution
4096 × 2048 px, about 5.2 km per pixel at the equator (232 m/px product, reduced to 1 km/px on Commons, then resampled here)
Processing
The colors are artificial: they are applied over a mosaic of Viking Orbiter images and are not a measurement of surface color. Resampled here with a Lanczos filter and saved as JPEG. No lighting is added on this page.

Real vs. approximated

Real

  • Both base maps are mosaics of orbital spacecraft images.
  • Lunar Module, Surveyor 3, Apollo 15 retroreflector and Apollo 17 rover positions are measured from LRO images.
  • The InSight position is measured from a georeferenced HiRISE image.
  • The Mars Pathfinder position is the center of a HiRISE image in which the lander is identified at the center.
  • Viking 1 and Phoenix are at the published landing coordinates of landers that never moved.

Approximated

  • The Apollo 14 golf balls, Apollo 16 photograph and waste bags have no surveyed position and are placed at the Lunar Module of their site.
  • The Spirit position is published only to 0.1° (about 6 km).
  • The Opportunity position is the center of a HiRISE image that contains the rover, not a surveyed rover coordinate.
  • Sojourner is shown at the Mars Pathfinder lander, because no latitude and longitude of its final position was found.
  • Ingenuity is shown at the Perseverance landing site, because no primary source for its final position could be opened.
  • Mars colors are artificial, and the lunar map beyond 70° latitude is filled from monochrome altimeter albedo.
